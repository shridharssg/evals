4. THE PROBLEM: HOW MOST PEOPLE TEST LLM APPLICATIONS
====================================================================

Typical approach:

1. Build an LLM application.
2. Ask a few questions.
3. Receive reasonable answers.
4. Assume the application works.

The instructor calls this:

VIBE TESTING

Definition:

Casually trying an LLM application with a few prompts and judging
its quality based on intuition or feeling.

Example:

"I asked 5 to 10 questions. The answers looked good.
Therefore the system must be working."

Problems with Vibe Testing:

• Informal
• Subjective
• Not repeatable
• Not suitable for production systems

It may be acceptable for personal projects but not for
production-grade applications.

5. CASE STUDY #1 - AIR CANADA CHATBOT
====================================================================

Scenario:

A customer asked Air Canada's chatbot about bereavement fare discounts.

The chatbot incorrectly stated:

• Buy the ticket now.
• Receive the discount later as a refund.

Actual policy:

• Discount had to be applied before purchase.
• Refund was not available later.

Outcome:

• Customer relied on chatbot advice.
• Customer was denied the refund.
• Customer sued Air Canada.

Air Canada's defense:

They argued that the chatbot was a separate entity.

Court decision:

The chatbot was part of the company's website and therefore the company
was responsible for its output.

Result:

• Company lost the case.
• Reputation damage occurred.

Lesson:

The chatbot should have been evaluated properly before deployment.


====================================================================
6. CASE STUDY #2 - CHEVROLET DEALERSHIP CHATBOT
====================================================================

Scenario:

A dealership chatbot was manipulated through prompt-jailbreaking.

User behavior:

• Convinced the chatbot to obey all future instructions.
• Asked whether a car could be sold for $1.

Chatbot response:

• Agreed to the request.
• Produced a binding-looking offer.

Outcome:

• Screenshots spread online.
• Significant negative publicity occurred.

Lesson:

Proper safety and evaluation testing could have identified this issue.


====================================================================
7. CASE STUDY #3 - HALLUCINATED LEGAL CASES
====================================================================

Scenario:

A lawyer used ChatGPT to find previous legal cases related to airline
injury incidents.

What happened:

• ChatGPT generated non-existent legal cases.
• Case names, facts, and details were hallucinated.
• The lawyer failed to verify them.
• The fake cases were presented in court.

Outcome:

• Errors were discovered.
• Monetary penalties were imposed.
• The episode became widely discussed.

Lesson:

LLM outputs must always be evaluated and verified.


====================================================================
8. KEY LESSON FROM ALL THREE CASE STUDIES
====================================================================

Evaluation is critical.

Deploying LLM applications without evaluation can lead to:

• Incorrect information
• Financial loss
• Legal issues
• Reputation damage
• Unsafe behavior

9. WHY IS EVALUATING LLM APPLICATIONS DIFFICULT?
====================================================================

The instructor compares traditional software testing with
LLM application evaluation.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
A. SOFTWARE SYSTEMS ARE DETERMINISTIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition:

The same input produces the same output.

Example:

2 + 2 = 4

The output is always identical.

Testing becomes straightforward.


--------------------------------------------------------------------
B. LLM APPLICATIONS ARE PROBABILISTIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition:

The same input may generate different outputs.

Example:

Question:
"What is overfitting in machine learning?"

Different users or different times may receive
slightly different answers.

All of them may still be valid.

This makes testing much more challenging.

10. MULTI-DIMENSIONAL EVALUATION
====================================================================

Traditional software usually focuses on correctness.

LLM applications require evaluation across multiple dimensions.

Possible dimensions include:

• Factual Accuracy
• Completeness
• Tone
• Groundedness
• Latency
• Cost
• User Experience

Different applications may require different evaluation criteria.
